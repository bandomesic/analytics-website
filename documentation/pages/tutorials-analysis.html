Before you start

The object-data overview needs no setup. Complete these once for hands-on tracking tutorials; reporting tutorials can use a site that already has data.

  1. 1
    Add your website

    Open Sites, select Add site, and enter the exact public URL including https://.

  2. 2
    Install the base tracker

    Copy the single script shown on the site page into your website. Most tutorials need no further website deployment; use a stable existing selector for interaction rules.

  3. 3
    Confirm the connection

    Visit your website once, return to the site page, and wait until the status says Tracker connected.

Keep two tabs open: this tutorial and your Privacy Analytics workspace. The examples use sample paths and names; replace them with real values from your website. Owners and administrators can create rules, sources, and dashboards; viewers can follow the analysis steps.

Tutorial 13

Read a campaign beyond the visit count

Use UTM tags and one consistent date range to see whether campaign traffic did anything useful.

◷ Self-paced
Step 1 of 6

Tag one destination consistently

Use a real landing page and standard campaign parameters in the link you distribute.

  1. Choose one landing page, such as /pricing.
  2. Append utm_source, utm_medium, and utm_campaign.
  3. Use one spelling for the same campaign across channels.
  4. Test the link in a browser without including personal data in parameters.
Example/pricing?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=autumn_launch
Step 2 of 6

Verify attribution

A test click should appear in the right campaign after collection and aggregation.

  1. Open your tagged link once on the tracked website.
  2. In Privacy Analytics, open Acquisition.
  3. Choose a date range containing that visit.
  4. Find the source, medium, and campaign in the campaign table.
  5. If nothing appears, first check that the tracker connected on the landing page.
TimingRealtime activity may appear before the aggregate Acquisition table updates.

Step 3 of 6

Keep the period the same

Review landing-page engagement and outcomes for the same dates before deciding the campaign worked.

  1. Note the selected date range in Acquisition.
  2. Open Engagement for the landing page and Goals & funnels for your outcome.
  3. Check visits, engagement, and conversion count together.
  4. If available, use Explore to filter the campaign or source rather than comparing unrelated totals.
Avoid a false conclusionA visit increase alone does not prove that the campaign caused more conversions.

Step 4 of 6

Check the link that was actually shared

A correct example URL does not guarantee every campaign link is tagged the same way.

  1. Open the final link from an email or ad, not only your draft.
  2. Check redirects preserve the UTM values on the landing page.
  3. Compare source, medium, and campaign spelling across distributed links.
Check before continuingCase or spelling differences can split one campaign into multiple report rows.

Step 5 of 6

Separate campaign signal from site totals

A site-wide conversion count can include visitors from every channel.

  1. Use campaign rows in Acquisition for reach.
  2. Use a filtered Explore view when comparing outcomes for that source or campaign.
  3. Keep the same period and metric definition throughout.
Check before continuingDo not attribute all site conversions to a single campaign without a matching filter.

Step 6 of 6

Write a useful finding

Record both the traffic and the outcome, with one caveat the next reviewer should know.

  1. Write down campaign visits and conversions for the selected period.
  2. Check whether internal or bot traffic affected the result.
  3. For a before-and-after view, explicitly choose Previous period in the comparison selector; comparison starts off.
  4. Decide what to repeat or change in the next campaign.
You are done whenYou can name the campaign, dates, visits, outcomes, and one limitation—not just a traffic number.

Tutorial 14

Compare a saved segment

Save one reusable filter, apply it in Explore, and compare the same period fairly.

◷ Self-paced
Step 1 of 6

Write the comparison question

A segment is a reusable rule about traffic, not a list of identified people.

  1. Choose one question, such as whether mobile visitors use different acquisition sources.
  2. Pick a dimension with values you can recognize; this example uses Device.
  3. Decide which metric and date range you will compare before creating the rule.
Check before continuingYou can state the segment, metric, and period in one sentence.
✓Question
✓Dimension
3Metric and dates
Step 2 of 6

Save one precise rule

Use the Decision center form so the same definition can be reused.

  1. Open Decision center → Segments.
  2. Expand + Save a segment.
  3. Name it Mobile traffic, choose Device, Equals, and value mobile.
  4. Choose a color and select Save segment.
Check before continuingThe saved card shows Device equals mobile; a broad Contains rule would answer a different question.
✓Device
✓Equals
3mobile
Step 3 of 6

Read the saved card carefully

The card confirms the rule and shows a value for the current comparison context.

  1. Check the saved name, dimension, operator, and value.
  2. Read the displayed value and share together, without treating the share as a permanent audience size.
  3. If the rule is wrong, remove it and save the corrected definition.
Check before continuingThe card describes the exact audience you intended to compare.
✓Rule shown
✓Value shown
3Context checked
Step 4 of 6

Apply it in Explore

Explore can use the saved definition instead of rebuilding the filter each time.

  1. Open Explore for the same site.
  2. Choose Mobile traffic under Saved segment.
  3. Choose a metric such as Pageviews and a Break down by dimension such as Source.
  4. Run the exploration for your chosen date range.
Check before continuingThe rows now answer “where did mobile pageviews come from?” for the selected period.
✓Saved segment
✓Pageviews
3Source
Step 5 of 6

Compare with the unfiltered view

Keep the setup the same so the segment is the only changed condition.

  1. Record the mobile result and top sources.
  2. Set Saved segment back to No saved segment.
  3. Keep the same metric, breakdown, and dates.
  4. Compare patterns and absolute volume; overlapping segment rules should not be added together as if they were distinct users.
Check before continuingYou can explain both the filtered and unfiltered result without changing the denominator mid-comparison.
✓Same dates
✓Same metric
3One filter changed
Step 6 of 6

Keep the definition useful

A reusable segment needs a stable meaning when teammates revisit it.

  1. Give it a name that makes the dimension and match obvious.
  2. Recheck it after site or campaign naming changes.
  3. Remove an obsolete segment rather than reusing its name for a new audience.
You are done whenAnother teammate can select the saved segment and reproduce the comparison.
✓Clear name
✓Stable match

Tutorial 15

Investigate a changed metric

Validate the data, find the largest contributors, and record a decision.

◷ Self-paced
Step 1 of 7

Name the change before explaining it

Start with one metric and a period whose data is complete.

  1. Open the site report where you noticed the movement.
  2. Choose Pageviews, Visitors, Sessions, or Events as the metric to investigate.
  3. Record the current period and the comparison period in UTC.
  4. Phrase a question such as “Why did pageviews fall in the last 30 days?”
Check before continuingThe question names a measurable change rather than assuming a cause.
✓Metric
✓Two periods
3Question
Step 2 of 7

Check measurement health

A broken collection or rollup can look like a real traffic decline.

  1. Open Data quality for the same site.
  2. Review Collection and processing and any Source freshness warning.
  3. If processing is delayed, resolve that first or wait for the period to complete.
Check before continuingYou have enough trustworthy coverage to compare the two periods.
✓Collection
✓Processing
3Coverage
Step 3 of 7

Start an investigation

The Decision center ranks the dimensions that contributed most to a change.

  1. Open Decision center → Investigations.
  2. Expand + Start an investigation.
  3. Enter the question, select the exact Metric, and choose Break down by, such as Source.
  4. Choose 7, 30, or 90 days, then select Analyze change.
Check before continuingThe investigation uses the same metric and intended length as your original question.
✓Question
✓Metric
3Breakdown
Step 4 of 7

Read the contributors

The result compares the current period with the previous one.

  1. Read the previous and current totals before the percentage change.
  2. Inspect the ranked findings and note which labels contributed most to the movement.
  3. Treat “New” or a sparse baseline cautiously; ranking describes contribution, not causation.
Check before continuingYou can name the largest contributor and its direction without overstating why it changed.
✓Totals
✓Ranked labels
3Limitations
Step 5 of 7

Cross-check the explanation

A source shift, campaign, release, or filter may explain the same movement differently.

  1. Open Explore with the same metric and a matching date range.
  2. Check a second dimension when useful, such as Page or Campaign.
  3. Review annotations and known launches for the period.
  4. If traffic quality or a tracking rule changed, document that before calling it user behavior.
Check before continuingAt least one independent view supports or limits the finding.
✓Explore
✓Annotations
3Tracking changes
Step 6 of 7

Record the action beside the evidence

A finding becomes useful when a teammate owns a next step.

  1. In Decision center → Decision log, expand + Record a decision.
  2. Enter a specific action, Owner, and Due date.
  3. Choose this investigation in Evidence.
  4. Use Summary to record what will change, why, and how success will be checked.
Check before continuingThe decision has an owner, a due date, and a link to the investigation.
✓Action
✓Owner
3Evidence
Step 7 of 7

Close the loop later

An investigation can end before the business outcome is known.

  1. Use Close investigation when the explanation is sufficient.
  2. Leave the decision open until the action is complete or intentionally stopped.
  3. After the due date, compare the same metric in a new complete period and add follow-up context.
  4. Use Mark resolved when the decision is complete.
You are done whenSomeone can trace the signal, evidence, action, and later result.
✓Close investigation
✓Check outcome
3Resolve decision

Tutorial 16

Explain a change with an annotation

Mark a release or incident and compare the surrounding trend.

◷ Self-paced
Step 1 of 6

Choose one event worth remembering

An annotation should explain a real change in your site or reporting context.

  1. Pick a launch, campaign, incident, or experiment with a known date.
  2. Write down what changed and which metric you expect it might affect.
  3. Avoid claiming the annotation proves causation.
Check before continuingYou have a specific date and a factual description, not a guess about results.
✓Known date
✓Concrete change
3Question
Step 2 of 6

Open the correct timeline

Annotations are created in Insights for one site.

  1. Open the site and choose Insights.
  2. Set a reporting period that includes the event date.
  3. Find Annotations and Mark the timeline.
Check before continuingThe date you need is within the selected report period.
✓Insights
✓Date range
3Timeline
Step 3 of 6

Write a useful note

A future teammate should understand the change without asking you.

  1. Set Date to the day the change happened in UTC.
  2. Use a short Title such as Pricing page launched.
  3. In What changed?, name the release and relevant audience or pages without including personal data.
  4. Choose a Marker color and select Add annotation.
Check before continuingThe annotation describes an observable change, not a conclusion about its effect.
✓Date
✓Title
3Context
Step 4 of 6

Read the trend around it

Use the marker as context while looking at the data.

  1. Confirm the note appears under Annotations.
  2. Look at the trend before and after the marked date.
  3. Keep the same date range and metric while checking a related report.
Check before continuingYou can say what moved and when, while keeping the reason tentative.
✓Marker visible
✓Same metric
3Before and after
Step 5 of 6

Check competing explanations

Traffic can change for reasons unrelated to the marked release.

  1. Look at campaigns, sources, and other known site changes in the same period.
  2. Check Data quality if volume or processing also changed.
  3. Use a comparison period only if both periods have comparable coverage.
Check before continuingYour written finding mentions at least one alternative explanation or limitation.
✓Other changes
✓Data quality
3Coverage
Step 6 of 6

Keep the timeline accurate

Annotations are most useful when they remain factual and current.

  1. Correct the record through a new note if the original explanation changed.
  2. A manager can use Remove on an obsolete annotation.
  3. Share the report date, metric, and caveat with the team.
You are done whenA teammate can interpret the movement and trace the real-world event behind the marker.

✓Remove obsolete
